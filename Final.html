Statistical Test Selector

Start Here: What are you comparing?

One-Sample Test: What kind of data?

One-Sample Quantitative: Is your data approximately normal?

(If $n \ge 30$, you can usually assume normality by the Central Limit Theorem. If $n < 30$, your data should look "bell-shaped".)

One-Sample Quantitative (Normal): Is the population standard deviation ($\sigma$) known?

(This is rare. You almost always use the *sample* standard deviation, $s$.)

Two-Sample Test: Are the groups independent or paired?

Two Independent Samples: What kind of data?

Two Independent Samples (Quantitative): Is data approximately normal in both groups?

(If $n_1 \ge 30$ and $n_2 \ge 30$, you can usually assume normality.)

Two Independent Samples (Normal, Quantitative): Are the population $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$ known?

(Again, this is rare. You almost always use the sample $s_1$ and $s_2$.)

Two Paired Samples: Are the *differences* approximately normal?

(Find the difference for each pair. If $n_{\text{pairs}} \ge 30$ or the differences look bell-shaped, answer "Yes".)
